## The finding that forced the replan

`CLAUDE.md`'s rejected list carried this, marked non-negotiable:

> **`skip_finalize`** on batch members. Skips the step that produces the
> `chan_pending` gate I-1 depends on.

That reasoning is wrong. In `lnwallet/chanfunding/psbt_assembler.go` at
v0.19.3-beta, `PsbtIntent.Verify` ends:

```go
// In case we aren't going to publish any transaction, we now have
// everything we need and can skip the Finalize step.
i.PendingPsbt = packet
if !i.shouldPublish && skipFinalize {
        i.FinalTX = packet.UnsignedTx
        i.State = PsbtFinalized

        // Signal the funding manager that it can now continue with its
        // funding flow as the PSBT is now complete.
        i.signalPsbtReady.Do(func() { close(i.PsbtReady) })
```

and `funding/manager.go:2290` reads that channel with `case nil:` — *"Nil error
means the flow continues normally now."*

So `skip_finalize` does not skip the gate. It skips handing LND a **signed**
transaction — which `lnwallet/wallet.go:1899` only stores at all when
`ShouldPublishFundingTX()` is true. With `no_publish` set, which I-1 requires on
every channel anyway, LND never needs a signature. `CompileFundingTx` still runs
(`wallet.go:1876`) because it "sets the actual funding outpoint in stone", and
the unsigned transaction is sufficient for that: all inputs are segwit — LND
enforces it in `Verify` — so witnesses do not move the txid.

**Consequence.** All *n* channels can reach `chan_pending` over an unsigned
transaction. Every channel in the batch becomes recoverable by force-close
*before any device has been asked for a signature.*

## What the app is

A wizard for the two things Sparrow and LND cannot do between them:

1. **Attribute the outputs.** A funding output is a P2WSH 2-of-2 with a peer. No
   signing wallet can tell you which peer it funds, at what amount, or that two
   were not swapped. Sparrow shows you payments to unrecognised addresses.
2. **Hold the I-1 gate.** Get all *n* channels to `chan_pending`, and only then
   let the transaction reach the network.

It builds nothing, holds no keys, selects no coins, derives no addresses, and
opens no socket except to your LND.

**It assumes you have** Sparrow (or comparable), LND, and a wallet with coins in
it — hot, cold, single-sig, multisig. Which of those does not matter to this
program and it should stop pretending otherwise.

## The sequence

```
1  pre-flight            peers reachable? anchor reserve sufficient?    [LND only]
2  open n streams        psbt_shim + no_publish              ← clock A starts
3  print the plan        peer · alias · address · amount · policy
4  build in Sparrow      enter the recipients, pick coins and fee, Save PSBT
5  verify + psbt_verify  outputs match the plan; skip_finalize; txid pinned
6  n × chan_pending      ← GATE OPEN. clock A stops, clock B starts
7  sign in Sparrow       no ten-minute pressure
8  txid unchanged? → publish once, via LND
9  watch to confirmation, apply policies
```

**Steps 2–6 are the only part under the ten-minute clock, and they contain no
signing.** That is the whole point of the inversion.

### Step 4, concretely

```
channel 1  bitrefill    bcrt1q...9f4a   250,000 sat
channel 2  acinq        bcrt1q...c17b   250,000 sat

Enter these as recipients in Sparrow, choose your coins
and fee, then Save PSBT — do not sign yet.
```

Addresses are copy-pasted from the terminal, not typed. A mis-paste is caught at
step 5 by `plan.Verify` — `UnnamedOutput`, `MissingOutput`, `WrongAmount` —
**before** `psbt_verify` pins anything, so it costs a redo inside clock A rather
than a channel. That check is the safety net, and it already exists.

A PSBT skeleton was considered and rejected: Sparrow signs PSBTs, it does not
fund them. There is no "load this unfunded PSBT and choose coins for it" flow
outside Core's `walletcreatefundedpsbt`, and removing Core is the point.

## The two clocks

These were conflated in an earlier draft. They are different clocks with
different owners and different consequences.

**Clock A — ten minutes, the peer's, covering steps 2 to 6.**
`chanfunding.DefaultReservationTimeout = 10 * time.Minute`. Our node never
expires one: `pruneZombieReservations` explicitly skips PSBT reservations
("we don't want to expire PSBT funding reservations… the remote peer is likely
going to cancel them after some idle time anyway"). The peer is the responder,
their reservation is not a PSBT one, so theirs does expire. It **stops at
`chan_pending`**, when the reservation completes and leaves `activeReservations`.

Ten minutes is LND's default. A CLN or Eclair peer has its own, and any peer can
reconfigure. Design against it as a convention, not a guarantee.

**Clock B — 2016 blocks, about two weeks, covering steps 6 to confirmation.**
`waitForTimeout` counts `lncfg.DefaultMaxWaitNumBlocksFundingConf` from the
channel's broadcast height and `fundingTimeout` fires — "only returned for the
responder". The peer marks the channel `FundingCanceled` and forgets it.

**Blowing clock B is worse than blowing clock A.** Clock A expiring costs a
restart and nothing else. Clock B expiring means the peer has deleted their
state, and a funding transaction that confirms afterwards leaves coins in a
2-of-2 with a counterparty who no longer knows about the channel.

So the recovery screen must name clock B **in blocks** — "peers give up at block
887,412, about 13 days" — rather than saying there is time.

## The invariants

**I-1 · Publish only once every channel is already recoverable.** Unchanged in
intent and nearly free to hold now. `no_publish` and `skip_finalize` on every
channel; gate at *n* of *n* `chan_pending`; one publish. Verified in source and
proved on regtest — see "Proved on regtest" below.

**I-2 · Dissolved, and the reason is written down rather than quietly dropped.**
I-2 existed so that no external party could broadcast before the gate opened.
After the inversion there is no "before the gate opens": you hold no signatures
until step 7, and the gate closed at step 6. Sparrow holding a fully signed
transaction front-runs nothing. The m−1 signing dance that assisted mode
specified is deleted along with it.

**I-3 · The txid must not move after verification.** Now the *only* load-bearing
check on what comes back from Sparrow. LND pins the outpoint at step 5; a
returned transaction that hashes differently kills every channel in the batch.
Already implemented, already tested, and it inherits the weight I-2 used to
carry.

**I-4 · Nothing in this repository replaces a funding transaction.** Enforced by
authorship, which is all that ever enforced it. The `Replaceable` sequence-number
refusal is gone (item 6): Core 29's full-RBF is unconditional, so refusing a
transaction over a signal that changes nothing was a lint wearing an invariant's
clothes.

## What the verifier refuses, and what it merely reports

The split is the difference between a tool and a nanny.

**Refuses** — the transaction does not match the plan you approved, or an
invariant is broken: `UnnamedOutput`, `MissingOutput`, `DuplicateOutput`,
`WrongAmount`, `LegacyInput` (I-3, LND's own requirement), `DuplicateInput`,
`MismatchedUTXO`, `NoUTXOInfo`, and the txid pin.

**Reports** — your fee and change arrangements are yours: `ChangeMissing`,
`ChangeTooSmall`, `FeeTooLow`, `FeeTooHigh`. A batch with no change output has no
CPFP lever and therefore no exit but an out-of-band double-spend — that is worth
**saying**, and not worth refusing over.

*This paragraph used to say they would move to `Verification.Unchecked`. They did
not, and the reason is in "Item 6, as built": `Unchecked` names what the verifier
**could not establish**, and these are things it did. They went to a new
`Verification.Reports` instead.*

## Consequences worth stating before they are discovered

**The journal's states reorder.** Today: verified → signing → pending →
published. After: verified → **pending** → signing → published. A channel at
`chan_pending` with nothing signed becomes the ordinary mid-run state rather than
an edge case, and `AbortTarget`'s split follows it.

**Abort is cheap right through step 7.** Changed your mind, blew clock A, Sparrow
crashed: abandon *n* pending channels. Nothing published, nothing on chain,
nothing lost but the ceremony.

**Publishing stops being load-bearing.** The app publishes through LND's
WalletKit because LND is already there and re-broadcasts until the transaction
confirms. It is a convenience now, not a gate — you could equally broadcast from
Sparrow. Keeping it is worth one call site; pretending it is a safety mechanism
is not.

**A stuck batch is the operator's, and they have the tool.** Recovery names the
inputs and the txid; the operator double-spends one in Sparrow. `docs/design.html`
already documents the procedure. The app says what to do and does not do it.

**The harness still needs a stand-in for Sparrow.** The application drops Core,
but a regtest test still needs something to build and sign a funding transaction.
Core's wallet is the natural stand-in, so `internal/bitcoind` and the simulated
multisig cold wallet survive **inside `internal/regtestenv`** as test fixtures.
That is not a back door for directed mode; it is the harness playing the
operator's part.

## Fresh build, or modify in place?

**Modify in place.** The packages that survive are precisely the ones that were
expensive to get right and are verified against a running node: `internal/arm`
holds the I-1 gate observed at *n* = 3, `internal/combine` has seventeen
adversarial tests on inbound PSBTs, `internal/plan` has the verifier this whole
plan now puts at the centre. Rewriting those to avoid deleting `coldwallet` and
`server` would be discarding the tested core to spare the peripheral code.

Deletion is cheap and git makes it reversible. A rewrite throws away the one
thing this repository has that a new one would not: evidence.

Do it on a branch, and keep the tool working at every commit.

## Proved on regtest — 2026-08-25

Item 1 is done. `TestSkipFinalizeReachesChanPendingWithNothingSigned` in
`internal/arm/skip_finalize_regtest_test.go` passed twice in a row, run alone
against `wt-alice`, `wt-bob` and `wt-carol`, at LND v0.19.3-beta.

What it did: opened *n* = 2 funding streams with `psbt_shim` and `no_publish`,
had the harness's stand-in wallet build an **unsigned** batch transaction, called
`psbt_verify` with `skip_finalize: true` on each stream, and read the receipts.

```
psbt_verify(skip_finalize) accepted for 020001ac65e054aa…
psbt_verify(skip_finalize) accepted for 02ab9355308fbaf2…
chan_pending for 020001ac65e054aa… at 4df25010…6d11:1, with nothing signed
chan_pending for 02ab9355308fbaf2… at 4df25010…6d11:2, with nothing signed
2 of 2 channels recoverable by force-close, nothing signed, mempool clear
```

Both `chan_pending` messages named exactly the outpoint the funding address
resolves to in the unsigned transaction, so LND set the outpoint in stone from
the unsigned bytes — `CompileFundingTx` ran, as `wallet.go:1876` says it must.
`PendingChannels` listed both, which is the same fact arriving by a route that
does not depend on the stream: a channel in that list is a channel in LND's
channel database, put there by `CompleteReservation`, which is the call that
stores the peer's commitment signature. And the transaction was not in the
mempool, because it has no witnesses for anyone to have broadcast it with.

**Nothing in the test signs anything.** There is no `psbt_finalize`, no signing
round and no combine. That absence is the assertion.

The four source citations were re-checked against
`github.com/lightningnetwork/lnd@v0.19.3-beta` in the module cache and all four
read as quoted, including `funding/manager.go:2308` — `// Nil error means the
flow continues normally now.` followed by a bare `case nil:`.

Two things the run established that the source reading did not:

- **The receipts arrive in under a second.** Both channels reached
  `chan_pending` inside the 0.55 s the whole test takes. Clock A — the peers'
  ten minutes — covers steps 2 to 6, and steps 2 to 6 are now essentially free.
  The ten-minute window was the binding constraint on the old sequence because a
  cold-storage signing round sat inside it; it is not a constraint on this one.
- **Abandoning them needs the blunt flag.** LND refused
  `pending_funding_shim_only` on both, with *"channel … is not externally funded
  or not pending"* — the known `ThawHeight > 0` inference. So the abort path for
  a channel that reached `chan_pending` this way is exactly the one the existing
  code already takes, and the replan's "abort is cheap right through step 7"
  costs an `i_know_what_i_am_doing` confirmation, as it always did.

`CLAUDE.md`'s rejected-list entry on `skip_finalize` is now wrong on a running
node as well as in the source. Correcting it is item 2.

## Item 3, as built — 2026-08-26

`internal/arm` now runs the sequence this document specifies, and the journal
records it in that order.

**What replaced what.** `arm.Verify` returns a `*Verified` — the pinned txid and
the outpoint each stream's funding address resolves to — after sending
`psbt_verify` with `skip_finalize` to all *n* streams. `arm.Receipts` takes that,
reads the *n* `chan_pending` receipts, journals each one and exports the backups,
and is the gate. `arm.Finalize` and `finalizeOne` are gone, and so is every
`psbt_finalize` call in the build: after a `skip_finalize` verify LND's intent is
already `PsbtFinalized`, and both finalize entry points require `PsbtVerified`,
so the call would be refused with *"invalid state. got finalized expected
verified"*. A stub test counts the calls and requires zero.

**`arm.Publish` takes the bytes as a parameter now**, because after the inversion
this program never held them: the batch was armed over an unsigned transaction and
signed by something else. What kept I-1 in the type system was the raw transaction
in an unexported field; it is now the `chan_pending` receipts in an unexported
map, one per channel, that only `arm.Receipts` fills. Publish re-derives the txid
from the bytes it is handed and refuses any that do not hash to the pinned one —
I-3 checked against the transaction about to be broadcast rather than against a
struct field.

**Three journal changes.** `MarkVerified` no longer moves the run: verifying and
collecting the receipts are one contiguous stretch with no operator step between
them, so a run stays in `arming` until the gate opens, and the channel rows say
how far each member got. A new `RecordPinnedTxID` writes the txid *before* the
first `psbt_verify`, because that call starts a peer storing a commitment
signature against these outpoints. A new `MarkSigning` moves `armed` → `signing`
and refuses any other predecessor, which is what makes the state mean "every
channel is already at `chan_pending`" — the recovery copy depends on that and
nothing else derives it.

**`MarkPublishing` counts instead of trusting the state.** It used to require
`StateArmed`, which was itself the proof that `MarkPending` had counted every
channel. With `signing` in between, that proof would have become a property of the
whole package rather than of one function, so the gate now asks
`countChannelsNotIn(ChanPending)` directly and accepts `armed` or `signing`. It
also still requires the raw transaction, and `raw_tx` now means something precise:
the bytes reach disk immediately before the publish RPC and nowhere else, so their
presence is "we may owe a rebroadcast". An armed run with no `raw_tx` is ordinary.

**One window this does not close, and it is not new.** A process that dies between
a channel's `psbt_verify` and its receipt leaves a row saying `verified` for a
channel LND has probably already created; the shim cancel that follows reports
success for a channel still pending on the peer's side. Inside a run this cannot
happen — `arm.Receipts` asks `PendingChannels` whenever a receipt does not arrive
— but a crash never gets to ask. The pre-inversion sequence had exactly the same
gap between `psbt_finalize` and its receipt. Closing it needs a lookup
`Run.AbortTarget` cannot make: it has no client, and the journal cannot map a
pending channel point back to a pending channel id.

**Deliberately not done here.** `combine.ErrAlreadyFinalized` still refuses a
device that returns a finalized input, still citing I-2. Nothing in item 3
produces a fully signed inbound packet — `arm` never calls `combine.Merge`, and
`run`'s signers are still the harness's partial-signature halves — so settling it
now would mean either a check with no caller exercising the new path or deleting
one the current happy path relies on. It is item 4's, with the `--psbt` path, the
package comment and the caller in one commit. `journal.SignerPartial`'s comment
still names I-2 for the same reason.

**Proved on the cluster.** `TestTheBatchPublishesExactlyOnceAndOnlyAfterEveryChannelIsRecoverable`
takes *n* = 3 to `chan_pending` with nothing signed, watching the mempool
throughout the verifies and the receipts, then signs, then publishes once, then
confirms all three channels.
`TestLNDItselfRefusesSkipFinalizeWithoutNoPublish` opens a stream *without*
`no_publish`, asks for `skip_finalize`, and gets *"cannot set skip_finalize for
channel that did not set no_publish"* — then re-verifies the same stream
successfully, which is the evidence that LND checks before advancing the intent
and that a batch refused at step 5 is re-armable rather than lost.
`TestSkipFinalizeReachesChanPendingWithNothingSigned` still passes unchanged, and
still drives `FundingStateStep` directly so that it keeps proving the claim even
if `arm` and `journal` are both wrong.

## Item 4, as built — 2026-08-26

`run` no longer builds the batch transaction and no longer signs it. It prints
the recipients, waits for a file, verifies it, arms the batch over it, waits for a
signed file, and checks what came back.

**The new seam is `run.SigningWallet`, and it is not `run.Signers`.** Two calls:
`Built(ctx, []Recipient)` is step 4 and `Signed(ctx, unsigned)` is step 7. The
names say what comes back rather than what the wallet does, because this program
does neither. Two implementations ship: `run.FileWallet`, driven by
`winthistle run --psbt FILE`, which waits for that path and then for
`FILE-signed.psbt` beside it; and `webrun`'s page wallet, which asks two questions
through the same `Ask`/`Reply` seam every other browser adapter uses — a path that
came in over HTTP would be a browser choosing which file this process opens.

`Signers` was not reused, and could not have been. It hands out *m* devices each
returning a partial signature, which is a shape I-2 required and nothing requires
now; and it has no call meaning "give me a transaction you funded yourself", so
step 4 could not have been built on it however step 7 went. It survives for the
CPFP child, which is still a multi-device round.

**`combine` is now the acceptance check on an inbound PSBT**, and the collision
item 3 left open is settled rather than deleted. `combine.Accept(plan, base,
signed)` is the whole of `Complete` bar the merge: parse, refuse a moved txid
(I-3), refuse a rewritten UTXO, execute every witness against its own script,
re-verify against the plan, hand out the network serialization. It takes complete
witnesses, which is what Sparrow produces, *and* a complete set of partials, which
is what a wallet that does not finalize produces.

`combine.ErrAlreadyFinalized` still refuses a finalized packet — through `Merge`,
not through `Accept` — and the reason is no longer I-2. A merge unions partial
signatures; finalization discards them; so a device that finalizes on its own has
ended a round the other devices were still in and left them nothing to add to.
That is a fact about merging, not a claim about who may hold bytes, and it is
still true of the CPFP child. The same correction went into
`internal/signers`' package comment and its operator-facing copy, and into
`journal.SignerState`, which gained `SignerSigned` for the batch and kept
`SignerPartial` for the child. `prose.signerNote` was the only place a signer
state is rendered and its switch had no default, so an unrecognised value counted
in nothing and rendered as "0 returned a partial, 0 still awaited, 0 declined" —
a run reading as though nobody had been asked anything, on the highest-stakes
screen in the product. It has a default branch now.

**Two gates went, and neither was about the batch.** `setup.Check` read back a
human's verdict on the cold wallet's descriptor pair; the app no longer touches
those descriptors, so the refusal had no subject. `rehearsal.Gate` refused to arm
a batch whose signing round would be slower than
`limits.abort_after_signing_seconds`; it measured a window that no longer
contains signing, and it needed Core and *m* devices, which `run` no longer has.
Both packages still compile and still have their own tests. `run`'s package
comment named five gates and now names four, with `plan.Verify` first — which is
where it always belonged, because it is the one the tool exists for.

**One thing this replan did not anticipate, and it had to be decided here.** The
app does not build the transaction, so it does not know the change address — and
an output the plan does not name is an `UnnamedOutput` refusal, which would have
refused every transaction Sparrow builds. `plan.Change.Recognise` already existed
for exactly this and needed fingerprints from somewhere, so
`plan.RecogniseChangeIn` reads them out of the transaction's own inputs: the
master fingerprints on the inputs are the wallet that is about to sign, and an
output carrying those same fingerprints on branch 1 is that wallet paying itself.
It is the same evidence a hardware signer uses, and the report already marks such
an output "recognised by key origin rather than by address".

It is weaker than naming the script and is meant to read that way. The packet gets
to nominate its own change output, and the circle is small: the funding outputs are
checked by script and to the satoshi against addresses LND issued, so nothing here
can move a channel's money, and a wallet that lies about its own change address
has already taken the coins it is about to sign for. `run --change ADDRESS` names
the script instead, which is stronger, and is the answer for a wallet that writes
no key origins at all — that case is a refusal that says so rather than a plan
reported as broken.

**Proved on the cluster.** `TestTheFilePathDrivesTheWholeSequence` drives
`run --psbt` end to end through the real file transport: the harness reads the
recipients off the printed table the way an operator reads them off a terminal,
builds the transaction with `coldwallet.Build`, and — at step 7 — signs with both
halves and combines *outside* the app, so `combine.Accept` meets the input it
will actually get. `TestTheColdProbeRunsTheRealPathAndWithholdsStepNine` and the
browser-driven probe both still pass and both still terminate through the abort
path. `TestSkipFinalizeReachesChanPendingWithNothingSigned` and
`TestTheBatchPublishesExactlyOnceAndOnlyAfterEveryChannelIsRecoverable` are
untouched.

**One new refusal, and it is I-1.** `combine.Unsigned` refuses a packet at step 4
that carries any signature. Step 4 is before the gate: a wallet that signs there
leaves the operator holding a broadcastable funding transaction while nothing has
reached `chan_pending`, and Sparrow's broadcast button is two clicks from its
signing one. A transaction that reached the network at that moment would confirm
one 2-of-2 output per channel with no channel behind any of them. It is the last
place I-1 can still be defeated from outside, and it costs a redo inside clock A.

**Copy that had to change because the code made it false.** `internal/server`'s
409 refusal and its overview both explained the one-run-at-a-time rule as "the
second run's dress rehearsal builds a decoy over the same coins" — there is no
rehearsal, and the collision survives it: two batches built from one wallet spend
the same outputs. `screens.waitingOn` called every batch question a signing gate;
a batch now has three question shapes and none of them is one, so it tells them
apart the way `questionForm` already tells them apart — off the fields. And
`plan.Verify`'s report printed the recognised-change note beside a bech32m
address, which is 88 columns against a 78-column pane; it was a rare line until
recognition became the ordinary path.

Everything on the cut list is deleted. **28,267 lines removed and 846 added**, in
five commits, with `make test-unit` green after each and `make check` green at the
end.

| 2 | `internal/bump`, `internal/signers`, `settle/cpfp.go`, the three bump tables | 7,000 |

What survives: `arm` · `plan` · `combine` · `peers` · `reserve` · `settle` ·

**The order was changed, once, and the reason is worth recording.** The handoff
put `bump` first, on the grounds that it holds `run.Deps.Signers` and the rest.
It does — but `internal/server` carried a `KindBump` arm in eight files and a
whole bump screen, so deleting `bump` first would have meant surgically editing
11,000 lines that the next commit deleted wholesale. Nothing depends the other
way: `server` and `webrun` import `bump`, never the reverse.

### The three decisions

**1 · The fee rate is declared, not fetched.** `[fees] target_sat_per_vb`
replaces `floor_sat_per_vb`, `target_blocks` and `mode`; `run --fee-rate N`
overrides it. `internal/fees` is deleted.

The options were to ask the operator, to derive something from LND's relay floor,
or to drop the fee finding. Asking wins, and not only by elimination: this program
does not build the transaction and does not choose the fee, so what the verifier
needs is not an estimate at all but something to hold the built transaction to —
and "the rate you told me you were aiming at" is a stronger thing to check against
than a number this program looked up on the operator's behalf. It is also how
everything else here works. You declare the batch, it checks the transaction.

A rate that quietly becomes zero was never on the table. `config.Load` refuses a
file with no `target_sat_per_vb`, naming I-4 in the refusal, and `plan.Build`
still refuses a non-positive target — two independent refusals, and
`TestAMissingFeeRateIsRefusedRatherThanDefaultedToZero` holds the first.
`winthistle doctor` keeps a fee check that verifies nothing and says so, because
that screen is the last chance to notice a mis-typed fee before the evening.

**2 · There is no pre-flight, and the absence is stated where it is read.**
`testmempoolaccept` was Core's, and its production call site was in `armWindow`
between `combine.Accept` and the publish. What survives is narrower and is not
nothing: `combine.Accept` executes every input's witness against its own script,
which answers "will each input validate" more directly than a mempool test and
needs no chain data. What is lost is node policy — min relay fee, standardness,
ancestor limits.

`plan.Verify`'s `Verification.Unchecked` already named `testmempoolaccept` as the
thing that would answer that; it now says this build does not run it and dials no
Bitcoin node at all. The operator reads that at step 5, before anything is pinned.
The step-7 line where the call used to be says what was checked instead, so the
call did not vanish quietly.

**3 · `Method.CallSites` is 1.** Changed with the prose beside it, `CLAUDE.md`'s
rejected-list bullet and `docs/design.html`, in the commit that deleted
`bump.Publish`. What kept the two publish lines apart was the type system; with
one left, the number is the whole claim, which is a reason to guard `CallSites`
harder rather than to relax it.

### Two things that were not in the plan

**A retired-key mechanism.** This slice removes eleven configuration keys and two
whole sections, and the reader refuses every key it does not know — which is
right, and which would have made every removal read as a typo. `retiredKeys` and
`retiredSections` give a removed setting a sentence saying what happened to it:
`[bitcoind]` says the tool dials no Bitcoin node, `[limits]
abort_after_signing_seconds` says the window it bounded no longer contains a
signing round.

`abort_after_signing_seconds` is the one that had to be decided rather than
merely done. Nothing has enforced it since item 3. Keeping it means a key that
changes nothing, which `docs/design.html` calls worse than no key at all; removing
it breaks every existing `winthistle.toml`. Removing it wins, loudly.

**The harness owns its coin locks now.** The application's abort path releasing
Core's locks was also how every fixture got its coins back. Deleting it starved
the first `make check` with "Insufficient funds" from a wallet whose balance was
fine — found by the harness rather than reasoned about.
`Env.BuildPSBTPaying` registers the release itself.

## Item 6, as built — 2026-08-26

The last one, and it was small: about 300 lines against item 5's 28,750. Two
halves, unrelated arguments, and they landed together.

### Half one — four findings became reports

`ChangeMissing`, `ChangeTooSmall`, `FeeTooLow` and `FeeTooHigh` no longer refuse
a batch. Your fee and change arrangements are yours: this app does not build the
transaction, does not select the coins, and cannot size a change output for you.
All it can do is hold what you built to what you declared and say where the two
disagree — which it does, and then arms.

**They did not go to `Verification.Unchecked`, and that was the one real decision
in the item.** This document and `docs/design.html` both said they would.
`Unchecked`'s own doc comment says it "names what this verification could not
establish, so that a clean result is not read as a broader guarantee than it is",
and it renders under the heading **"Not checked here"**. *"Your change is 600 sat
and the floor is 12,350"* is something the verifier plainly did establish. Filing
it under that heading would have cost the heading the only thing it is for, and
that heading is what keeps a clean result honest.

So `Verification` grew a third list:

```go
Problems  []Problem  // refusals; OK() is len(Problems) == 0
Reports   []Finding  // established, not refused over
Unchecked []string   // could not be established at all
```

**`Finding` is a separate type from `Problem`, not the same one in a second
slice.** `Problem`'s doc comment says every problem is a refusal and there is no
severity here on purpose, and item 6's job was to keep that sentence true rather
than edit around it — a `Reports []Problem` would have falsified it on the spot.
Two types make the split something the compiler knows: a demoted finding cannot
be appended to `Problems` by accident, so `OK()` cannot come to depend on a grade
somebody set wrong. Nothing grew a severity field.

The plumbing got simpler on the way. `plan.Verify` used to thread an
`add func(Code, string, string, string)` closure through five `check*` functions,
which would have meant threading a second. Both are methods on `*Verification`
now — `v.refuse(...)` and `v.note(...)` — and the five signatures each lost a
parameter.

**Four codes stayed refusals, deliberately.** `ChangeAmbiguous`: two outputs that
look like change is an *attribution* failure, the same family as `UnnamedOutput`,
and attribution is the product. `NoFee`: LND refuses it itself at `psbt_verify`,
so reporting it would arm a batch LND is going to reject. `Unsizable`: with no
size there is no fee rate, so there is nothing to report *about*. `LegacyInput`:
I-3 and LND's own requirement, and nothing pre-excludes a legacy coin now that
Sparrow picks them.

**Both places that print a verification print the reports.** Step 5's report is
printed unconditionally and `Verification.Report()` renders them under a heading
of their own, **"Reported, not refused"**, between "What is wrong" and "Not
checked here". Step 7's recheck prints nothing on success — a clean re-run is not
news — so it prints `recheck.Reported()` explicitly. That is not decoration: the
size is *exact* at step 7 rather than an upper bound, because every witness is
present, so a rate that sat inside tolerance at step 5 can land outside it there
and step 7 is the first place anybody could know. A demoted finding nobody prints
would be worse than the refusal it replaced. `Summary()` carries them on both
branches too, so a log never says "matches the plan" about a batch with no change
output.

**A batch with no change output now arms with no further prompt**, which is the
item rather than a gap in it. No confirmation gate was added and none should be:
a tool that refused your transaction over your own change arrangements would be
claiming an authority it gave up at step 4.

### Half two — the `Replaceable` refusal is gone

`plan.Code` has no `Replaceable` member, `plan.MaxNonReplaceableSequence` is
deleted, and `checkInputs` no longer looks at a sequence number.

**This did not relax I-4, and the two are the same diff to a fast reader.**
I-4 is enforced by authorship and always was: only we can sign our inputs, and
no code path in this repository replaces a funding transaction. The lint judged a
signal Core ignores — full-RBF is unconditional in Core 29, verified live —
so removing it took nothing away. `InputView.Sequence` still records what each
input said, so the fact is on the report and simply not judged, and the comment
where the refusal used to stand says all of this at the one place somebody would
put it back.

`MaxNonReplaceableSequence` was deleted rather than kept as a documented
constant. Four `internal/arm` fixtures used it to build a *non*-replaceable
transaction and now say `wire.MaxTxInSequenceNum - 1`, which is what they meant.
Keeping an exported constant named after a refusal that no longer exists would
have been an invitation.

`internal/combine`'s second line of defence survives with a better reason.
`TestASignerThatMadeTheTransactionReplaceableIsRefused` is now
`TestASignerThatChangedASequenceNumberIsRefused`: `combine.Merge` still refuses
an edited sequence as `ErrDifferentTransaction`, and the test's tail now asserts
that the plan verifies the rebased transaction happily **and that its txid moved**
— which is I-3, and is what actually catches this.

### The copy, and there was more of it than the three strings

`CLAUDE.md` named three strings still framing a stuck batch in custody language.
A sweep found the same framing in eight more places, including the most
operator-facing one of the lot: `plan.checkChange`'s own `ChangeMissing` detail
text. All of them are rewritten to the rule **name what is missing (the lever),
never imply what is not (risk)**. Nothing is at risk in a stuck batch — the coins
are yours, unspent, in a transaction only you can sign — and what is missing is
the exit.

Also fixed on the way, because half two falsified them: `report.go`'s "a
transaction with any input below sequence 0xfffffffe is refused" plan bullet,
`combine.Recheck`'s doc comment ("the fee rate inside tolerance … nothing
replaceable"), `doctor`'s "passes step 5" fee lines, `run --fee-rate`'s help
text, and `config`'s `allow_rbf` refusal — which still described a CPFP child
`winthistle bump` builds, deleted in item 5.

`internal/plan/plan.go`'s no-change refusal survives with a different subject. It
no longer says change is the only way to accelerate a stuck batch; it says the
plan has no way to *identify* the change output, which is an attribution failure
and the check the program exists for. `run --change ADDRESS` is the answer.

**Two strings were left alone**, in `internal/regtestenv/coldwallet/build.go`.
They carry the old framing, they are harness-only — the stand-in for Sparrow
refusing to build a fixture — and item 6 did not falsify them.
